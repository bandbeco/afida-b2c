# Leads Proposal (August 2026)

**Source framework:** Alex Hormozi, *$100M Leads* (2023).
**Prepared:** 2026-08-09, from a full read of the book.
**Companions:** this completes a trilogy. The [Grand Slam Offer Proposal](/proposals/grand-slam-offer-2026-08.md) covers what the first offer is; the [Money Model Proposal](/proposals/money-model-2026-08.md) covers what happens after the first purchase; this document covers the layer in between and before: how strangers find out the offer exists at all.

## The framework in one paragraph

Hormozi defines advertising as "the process of making known", and its true output as the engaged lead: a person who has shown interest, not merely a contactable name. Engaged leads are bought with a lead magnet (a complete free solution to a narrow problem that reveals the problem your core offer solves), and there are exactly four ways to deliver the message, a 2×2 of audience (warm or cold) by format (one-to-one or one-to-many): warm outreach, posting free content, cold outreach, and paid ads. Whatever works gets scaled with "more, better, new" (in that order, and under roughly £1M profit the answer is almost always more), then multiplied through four lead getters who advertise on your behalf: customers (referrals), employees, agencies, and affiliates. Paid ads earn the right to scale only when lifetime gross profit is at least 3× acquisition cost and the first 30 days of cash cover the cost of acquiring the customer.

## Where Afida stands

Mapping the Core Four plus lead getters onto today's reality:

| Channel | Afida today |
| --- | --- |
| Warm outreach (1-to-1, people who know you) | Unused. Warm audience exists: 99 online customers (86 lapsed), a 69-profile Klaviyo list, and the offline trade book, which nobody contacts one-to-one systematically |
| Post free content (1-to-many, warm) | The only channel actively worked: blog is 74% of GSC impressions, Move 1 CTAs wired, 89 Outrank drafts queued (see [B2B organic plan](/seo/b2b-organic-growth-plan-2026-07.md)) |
| Cold outreach (1-to-1, strangers) | Unused, despite the lead monitor (unmerged `lead-monitor` branch) already building the exact list the book tells you to scrape: newly registered food businesses |
| Paid ads (1-to-many, strangers) | Small Google Ads spend; campaign tree agreed 2026-05 but deliberately not scaled |
| Customers as lead getters | No referral ask anywhere: not at checkout, not in email, not in trade conversations |
| Affiliates/partners | None, despite partner-shaped relationships (Enviropack, coffee roasters and other café suppliers adjacent to the same buyer) |
| Employees / agencies | Not applicable at current scale |

Two conclusions fall out. First, Afida advertises through a fraction of one quadrant; three of the four channels, and all four lead getters, are at zero, and the zeros are the cheap ones. Second, the book independently re-derives the money-model build order: its diagnostic says a business whose LTGP:CAC sits below 3:1 has a business-model problem, not an advertising problem, and at 86-of-99 one-time buyers Afida's lifetime gross profit is a single £96 order. Scaling any paid channel before the continuity plays land would be paying to fill a leaking bucket. The free channels below are exempt from that gate precisely because they cost time, not CAC.

## The unit of account: a sample request is an engaged lead

The book's central accounting change: stop counting traffic, start counting engaged leads. Afida's natural engaged-lead event is the sample request. Free samples (customer pays delivery) are literally the book's prescribed lead-magnet type for consumables, and "a person who pays with their time now is more likely to pay with their money later" is the sample box's whole theory. Every play below therefore points its call to action at the sample box (curated per avatar, per the Grand Slam Offer work), not at the catalogue. The catalogue is the core offer; the box is the advertisement.

## The plays, in priority order

### 1. Warm outreach over the trade book and past buyers (free, start now)

The book's first prescription for any business under scale: one-to-one messages to people who already know you, personalised, with a free offer, at a fixed daily count. Afida's warm audience is small but unusually warm: offline trade accounts with years of relationship, plus 86 lapsed online buyers whose consumption ran out months ago. The motion is Afida leadership's, not code: a personal message ("Are you still sorting your packaging for the café?", the book's nine-word re-engagement pattern), the sample box or the [rollover offer](/proposals/money-model-2026-08.md) as the give, and "who else do you know running a café?" as the ask. The book's benchmark is roughly 1 customer per 100 warm reach-outs with 3 follow-ups; at Afida's relationship depth it should do far better. Ten per day, every working day, beats a hundred once.

### 2. Ask for referrals at the moment of purchase (customers are lead getter #1)

Cafés know cafés: suppliers, trade groups, the shop three streets over. Today nothing anywhere asks. Three mechanics from the book, in Afida form:

* **Two-sided, product-denominated.** "Give a free sleeve of lids, get a free sleeve of lids" beats cash: the incentive is the product, so it self-selects qualified referrals, and both sides' cost is a fraction of the £96 AOV. Place it on the order-confirmation page beside the continuity card, and in the post-delivery Klaviyo email.
* **Referral as negotiation chip.** In trade quoting, trade the discount that would have been given anyway for a three-way introduction to two or three named businesses. Never discount for nothing.
* **Scoreboard.** The book's referral growth equation (% customers referring monthly minus % lapsing monthly) is the number to watch once reorder data is richer; referrals above lapse is compounding growth without ad spend.

### 3. Cold outreach: give the lead monitor its last mile

The lead monitor already diffs FSA registrations into a weekly digest of new food businesses: a list of strangers in their hyper-buying window, which is exactly the list the book says to build. What is missing is the outreach motion on top. The message is the new-opening kit from the [Grand Slam Offer Proposal](/proposals/grand-slam-offer-2026-08.md) (curated sample box, quantity prescription, next-day top-up promise), delivered as the book prescribes cold outreach: personalised with one or two researched details (their cuisine, their opening announcement), lead with the free give not the pitch, written at a plain reading level (the book's test: rewriting below 3rd-grade level lifted responses ~50%), followed up on day 1, day 2 and day 7, and the list re-run after 3 to 6 months. Book benchmark: roughly 3% of a cold list becomes engaged leads; even 30 openings contacted a week is a steady sample-box pipeline. This is the business case that finally justifies merging the `lead-monitor` branch.

### 4. Partner white-labelling (affiliates, scaled to Afida size)

The book's cheapest affiliate integration: non-competing businesses that already sell to your avatar give away your lead magnet with their own product. Afida's avatar buys from coffee roasters, POS vendors, food-hygiene consultants and wholesalers before it ever searches for packaging. Two giveable assets exist or are one session's work: the UK packaging-compliance one-pager (a Grand Slam stack item) offered white-label ("put your logo on it"), and the sample box itself ("every new wholesale customer of ours gets a free eco-packaging starter box from Afida"). Pay in product, not cash, per the book's match-the-incentive rule. Pilot with 3 to 5 partners from existing relationships before building anything.

### 5. Content: keep the SEO plan, add the book's discipline

The organic plan stands; the book adds three corrections rather than a new channel. Keep the give:ask ratio deliberate now that Move 1 CTAs exist (give in public, ask in private). Add the missing genre: "how [a real café] switched", the book's "how I, not how to" story, which doubles as the testimonial ammunition every other play needs. And build the greatest-hits library: the handful of pages that convert become the links pasted into every warm and cold outreach message, which is how content quietly finances the one-to-one channels.

### 6. Paid ads: keep the gate, adopt the operating system for later

The hold on scaling ads stays; it is now triple-confirmed (money model, offer work, and this book's LTGP:CAC rule). What to take today is the operating system for when the gate opens: ads that call out the avatar by label ("Café owners:"), landing pages that visually match the ad (the avatar offer pages from the Grand Slam proposal, not the homepage), one test per week with a written log, kill rules budgeted at 2× the 30-day cash a customer brings, and budget reverse-engineered from a customer goal plus 20%. Lookalike targeting needs a larger customer list than 99; that arrives as plays 1 to 4 work.

## What to ignore from the book

The 100-per-day, 100-day volume prescriptions assume outreach is someone's full-time job; at founder scale the transplant is the consistency, not the number (a fixed daily count, kept daily). The employees and agencies chapters are premature at current revenue, except two keepers for the first marketing hire whenever that happens: document-demonstrate-duplicate training, and hiring experience over potential. The whisper-tease-shout launch sequence and the Dropbox/PayPal case studies are consumer-scale; their two-sided-incentive mechanics transplant, their magnitudes do not.

## Who does what

The channel motions (warm messages, trade-quote referral chips, partner conversations) belong to Afida leadership, who own the relationships. The code work belongs to the dev retainer: referral prompt on the confirmation page and post-delivery email, outreach layer on the lead monitor (contact fields, message templates, sent/replied tracking on Lead records), the white-label compliance PDF, and engaged-lead counting (sample requests by source).

## How this slots into the build order

The [Money Model Proposal](/proposals/money-model-2026-08.md) build order is untouched; this proposal adds the free lead channels alongside it rather than ahead of it:

1. **Now, alongside the continuity work:** play 1 (warm outreach can start this week with no code) and play 2's confirmation-page referral ask (same surface as the continuity bonus card, one combined change).
2. **Next:** play 3 shipped with the lead-monitor merge and the new-opening kit; play 5's case-study content folded into the next content batch.
3. **Then:** play 4 partner pilot, once the kit and compliance one-pager exist to give away.
4. **Last and still gated:** play 6, scaling paid ads, only after repeat purchase works and 30-day cash covers CAC.

## Measurement

One new counter and three ratios. The counter: engaged leads per week (sample requests plus outreach replies), split by channel, which becomes the top line of the existing scoreboard. The ratios: referral rate vs lapse rate (play 2), sample-to-first-order conversion (shared with the Grand Slam scoreboard), and LTGP:CAC, which must clear 3:1 before any ad budget rises. All four are cheap to pull monthly alongside the money-model repeat-purchase distribution.
