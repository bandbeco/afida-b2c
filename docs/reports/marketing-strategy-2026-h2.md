# Afida — Marketing Strategy (H2 2026)

_Prepared for Tariq · Drafted 2026-07-05 · Covers the next 6–12 months_

---

## The one-paragraph version

Afida is already spending real money on marketing (~£500/month with the ads agency, ~£6,000/year), but **we cannot currently prove what that spend returns** — in our own analytics, paid ads show £0 of attributed revenue. Meanwhile the channels that _do_ visibly make money (organic search bringing volume, and direct/repeat customers bringing the actual revenue) are growing steadily and cost almost nothing. So the strategy is simple and disciplined: **make the money we already spend accountable first, then compound the cheap channels that are working.** No new big bets, no new big budgets. Steady, provable growth on a lean footing.

---

## Where Afida is today (the honest diagnosis)

All figures from live analytics (Datafa.st + Stripe + Google Search Console), pulled July 2026.

- **Revenue is real and trending up.** Last 30 days: **£2,960 revenue, 31 orders**, conversion 1.02%. June was the best month on record. The direction is right.
- **The UK is essentially the entire business.** UK visitors convert; overseas visitors (US, Brazil, Singapore, India ~36% of all traffic) buy **nothing**, because we ship UK-only. Headline conversion looks weak only because it's diluted by people who literally can't check out.
- **Direct and repeat customers are the profit engine.** "Direct" traffic (people who already know Afida) is the **#1 revenue source** and by far the highest value per visitor. Returning visitors have climbed to ~18%. The brand is starting to compound.
- **Search brings visitors; it doesn't yet bring enough buyers.** Google sends the most visitors, but a lot of them land on informational or non-buying pages (straws, blog articles) and don't purchase. The commercial pages that _would_ sell sit on page 2–3 of Google — proven demand, lost purely to ranking position.
- **The one proven B2B win:** the page targeting **"sustainable packaging for restaurants"** ranks near the top of Google and converts. That's the template — trade buyers searching for supplies — and it's the thread this whole strategy pulls on.
- **Mobile has been fixed.** Six weeks ago mobile converted at a third of desktop. It's now level with (slightly ahead of) desktop and is the larger revenue source. A conversion problem that _was_ the top priority is now off the list.

**In one line:** Afida doesn't have a traffic problem or a conversion problem. It has an **accountability problem** (unmeasured spend) and a **relevance problem** (the right B2B buyers aren't finding the right pages yet).

---

## The strategy: four pillars, in priority order

### Pillar 1 — Make the £500/month accountable _(do this first)_

We are spending ~£6,000/year on Google Ads through the agency. In Afida's own analytics, that spend is attributed **£0 of revenue.** The true number is almost certainly not zero — ad clicks often convert later and get miscredited to "Direct," and our conversion tracking has known gaps we've been fixing. **But right now, nobody can prove the return either way, and that is not an acceptable place to be with £6k/year.**

This is the single highest-value thing we can do, because it plays to a strength no agency will offer: **an independent scoreboard.** The agency reports on its own numbers. We own the Stripe revenue and the site analytics. We're now positioned to reconcile the two.

**Actions:**
- Reconcile agency-reported ad conversions against **actual Stripe orders**, month by month.
- Close the remaining attribution gaps so paid, organic, and direct each get honest credit.
- Produce a simple monthly **blended scoreboard** for Tariq: what each channel cost and what it returned.

**Why it matters:** if even part of the £500/month isn't paying back, redirecting it is worth more than anything the free channels can do on a shoestring. And if it _is_ paying back, we'll finally be able to prove it and spend with confidence. Either outcome is a win.

### Pillar 2 — Compound organic search (the B2B buyer lane)

This is the durable, cheap, already-working engine. We keep doing what's begun: lifting Afida's **commercial pages** (soup containers, ice-cream cups, cutlery, NatureFlex, Vegware) from Google page 2 onto page 1, and replicating the **"[product] for restaurants / cafés"** template that already ranks and sells.

**Actions:**
- Continue the on-page work already in flight (category depth, FAQs, buying guides, internal links from the blog).
- Own the branded terms we're the official UK stockist for — **Vegware** and **NatureFlex** — which we currently rank for but don't win.
- Steer effort toward **buyer-intent** trade searches, away from informational blog traffic that doesn't convert.

**Why it matters:** organic compounds. It's the cheapest possible fit for a 4-hour-a-week, near-zero-budget reality, and it targets exactly the B2B café/restaurant buyer Afida wants.

### Pillar 3 — Grow the repeat/direct base (retention)

The quiet winner in the data. Direct and returning customers already drive the most revenue; this pillar deliberately feeds that base rather than chasing strangers.

**Actions:**
- Put the existing **email flows (Klaviyo)** to work: welcome, post-purchase, reorder reminders (trade customers reorder predictably).
- A **light, automated social presence** (see the separate 90-day social plan) — mainly to feed brand recall and act as a credibility signal for buyers vetting a supplier, not as a primary sales channel.

**Why it matters:** retaining a trade customer who reorders monthly is worth far more than winning a one-off consumer, and it costs a fraction of acquisition.

### Pillar 0 — Paid ads (already running, now measured, not managed by us)

The agency continues to run Google Ads. Our job is **not** to manage it — it's to hold it accountable (Pillar 1) and, once we can see true returns, advise Tariq on whether to sustain, cut, or redirect the £500/month.

## Who does what

- **Agency:** delivers the paid ad campaigns.
- **Laurent:** everything organic (search, content, retention automation) **and the scoreboard** that measures all channels, including the agency's.
- **Tariq:** sets the growth ambition, approves budget, decides on the paid spend once its return is proven.

Clean division: the agency spends, Laurent grows-and-measures, Tariq decides.

---

## Budget & effort (the reality this is built for)

- **Paid media:** ~£500/month, already committed, via the agency. This strategy makes it accountable; it does not add to it.
- **Organic/tooling budget:** ~£30/month discretionary. Enough for the odd tool or asset (e.g. self-hosted social, an image key). **No additional ad spend proposed** until Pillar 1 proves the funnel.
- **Hands-on time:** ~4 hours/week (Laurent). The whole plan is deliberately **leverage-first** — compounding channels (SEO), automation (email flows, auto-posting), and a small number of high-ROI plays — precisely because 4 hours can't sustain many manual channels.

This is a strategy sized honestly to what's available, not a wish list.

---

## What "steady growth" looks like (how we'll know it's working)

Tariq wants a reliable upward trend, not a spike. So we measure the **trend**, monthly:

- **UK revenue and orders** (month-on-month) — the primary line.
- **Blended return on the £500/month** — the Pillar 1 scoreboard.
- **Commercial-page rankings** (page 2 → page 1 progress on the B2B terms).
- **Repeat-customer share** (currently ~18%, we want it climbing).

**Near-term milestones**
- **~2 weeks:** first honest read on whether the ad spend is paying back (Pillar 1).
- **~mid-July:** measure the impact of the SEO work already shipped.
- **~90 days:** decide on paid spend with real numbers; assess the social trial.
