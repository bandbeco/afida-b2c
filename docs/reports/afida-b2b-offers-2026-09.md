# Afida.com: B2B offers, and what the game should actually give

A café owner does not want 5% off, a sleeve of cups they may not use, or a sample box they can already claim on `/samples`. They want stock that fits, arrives tomorrow, does not run out, and (if they are ready) has their name on it. This note starts from the live business, then names a few offers that match, then maps the stack game onto two prize sizes.

Companions: [growth plan](/reports/growth-plan-2026-2031.md), [money model](/proposals/money-model-2026-08.md), [grand slam](/proposals/grand-slam-offer-2026-08.md), [leads](/proposals/leads-2026-08.md), [game offer audit](/reports/stack-game-offer-audit-2026-09.md).

## 1. What afida.com actually is

Online store only (offline trade is a separate book). Figures from mid-2026 production data.

| Fact | Number | Why it matters for offers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Run-rate | ~£3k/month, trending up | Offers have to work at this scale; no 10,000-redemption coupons |
| AOV | ~£96 | A “win” that needs £100+ is basically “place a normal order” |
| Repeat | 86 of 99 customers bought once | The hole is continuity, not attraction |
| Mix | Cold cups (breadth), hot cups and Bella pots (revenue), also straws, napkins, lids, salad bowls | Any SKU-specific prize (cups) misses half the book |
| Traffic | UK converts; ~36% overseas cannot buy | Game players who are not UK trade are a leak |
| Competition | Identical Vegware SKUs vs Nisbets, Bunzl, Amazon | Cannot win on price. Offers must be incomparable |
| Margin | ~30–35% on resale; 45–60% on custom print | A prize that opens a print job is worth more than a prize that discounts stock |
| Welcome offer already on site | **10% off first order over £100** | For a **first** order, game 5% is worse than the cart. For anyone who has already bought, the 10% is gone — the game is the only on-site coupon they still have |

The store already has most of Hormozi’s machinery and almost none of it is composed into a named offer:

| Stage | Exists | Named as an offer? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attraction | Samples (pay delivery), 10% welcome, SEO | No. “Free samples” and “10% off” |
| Upsell | Compatible lids, quantity tiers, branded configurator, £100 free shipping | No. Features, not offers |
| Downsell | Nothing | — |
| Continuity | Reorder schedules, **one** live | Confirmation page is a membership pitch, not a bonus |

What a UK café actually buys Afida for, when they do: official Vegware/NatureFlex stockist, next-working-day (2pm cutoff), a human, honest compostability copy, and samples. What they do **not** buy Afida for: being cheaper than Nisbets.

The growth plan’s wedge, in one line: **custom print for the café’s own logo is the high-margin door into a full packaging account.** Print partner already exists. Blog already ranks for “custom printed coffee cups small orders.” Leadership still has to decide whether that revenue lives at Afida or Brand Brothers — any print-shaped prize waits on that call.

## 2. What a B2B buyer finds compelling (and what they do not)

Trade buyers of packaging are restocking, opening, or switching. Their dream is not “5% off kraft.” It is:

1. **Opening week goes smoothly** (new food business — starving crowd, already identified by the lead monitor).
2. **Never run out mid-service** (existing café — the 86 one-timers).
3. **The packaging looks like my brand** (the ones ready for print).
4. **I am not an idiot in front of my customers about compostability** (the honest-education niche).

They bounce on: percentages, SKU-specific freebies they don’t use, “win a sample” of something already free, and anything that requires a consumer-shaped checkout to feel the prize.

Hormozi, applied here without the theatrics:

- **Free beats discount.** “Delivery on us” or “setup on us” outsells “5% off.”
- **Bonuses of a different kind.** Don’t stack two percentages.
- **Never discount the same thing to buy the yes.** Trade a bonus for a commitment (reorder, print job, referral).
- **Product-denominated, but not SKU-denominated.** Pay in packaging they choose, not in cups.
- **The sample box is the lead magnet, not the trophy.** It is already free. Winning it looks like a sales call.

## 3. Offers worth naming (site-wide, not only the game)

These are the commercial offers afida.com is in a position to make. The game can *point at* two of them. The rest should live on confirmation, cart, and `/branding` whether the game exists or not.

### A. “Your Name On It” — first branded run, setup on us

- **Who:** A café that already knows their logo and is tired of generic cups/boxes/bags.
- **Promise:** We print your mark on the line you actually use. First-run origination/setup waived. You pay for the stock.
- **Why it works:** Print is the thing they cannot get from Nisbets in five minutes, it is SKU-flexible if the partner prints cups *and* boxes/bags, perceived value is hundreds of pounds, Afida’s cash cost is the setup not a warehouse of free cases, and a print customer becomes a packaging account (growth plan, explicitly).
- **Catch:** Confirm Brand Brothers vs Afida. Confirm the partner’s setup fee and which lines they print. If setup is ~£250–700, this is a real prize at a known cost.
- **Downsell if they freeze:** plain stock now, branded on the next cycle. Same thing for less money is forbidden; different thing is allowed.

This is the monthly-crown candidate.

### B. “Never Run Out” — scheduled reorder, this delivery free

- **Who:** Anyone who just placed a real order.
- **Promise:** Tick monthly/fortnightly on the lines you just bought; today’s carriage is on us (or a free case of lids/napkins/whatever they ordered — they pick).
- **Why it works:** Continuity is the hole (86 of 99). The machinery exists. The confirmation page currently asks with nothing in it for them. Hormozi’s dog-food template is this offer.
- **Not a game prize.** It belongs on the order-confirmation page. The game can *mention* it (“winners who stock up never run out”) but should not try to be the reorder system.

### C. “The Opening Week Kit” — new food businesses

- **Who:** Newly registered cafés/takeaways (lead monitor list).
- **Promise:** Curated sample of *their* type of service, a written quantity prescription, next-day top-up promise, compliance one-pager. Then: one-off or monthly.
- **Why it works:** Starving crowd, hyper-buying cycle, already specified in the grand-slam doc. The game is a poor fit for this avatar (they are on Companies House, not Instagram arcade). Keep this as outbound, not a cabinet prize.

### D. Round-pound credit — “£20 off any order this month”

- **Who:** Anyone. SKU-agnostic.
- **Promise:** Twenty pounds, not five percent. Works on pizza boxes, napkins, cups, mixed baskets.
- **Why it is only a *small* prize:** It is still a discount. It looks like money, which 5% does not. It must not undercut the 10% welcome on first orders — so either it *is* the welcome (play to unlock the existing 10%), or it is a *different* lever (delivery, setup, credit that stacks with nothing).
- **Maths:** 200 redemptions × £20 = £4,000/month worst case, too fat. Cap it (already 200 on the coupon) or keep it scarce (win screen, not a scrapeable code). Current 5% × £100 × 200 = £1,000/month. A £15–20 credit at a tighter cap (50/month) is the same budget and looks like a prize.

### E. “Delivery on this one, even under £100”

- **Who:** First-time trade-sized-but-cautious buyers.
- **Promise:** One free-carriage code, no £100 floor.
- **Why it can work:** Small orders barely carry their own shipping (growth plan). Waiving carriage once is a real gift, SKU-agnostic, and trains the next order toward the £100 threshold rather than pretending they already won a stocking discount. Cost to Afida is the postage, not 5% of a basket they might never place.

Do **not** lead with samples-as-prize, cup-sleeves, or a second percentage on top of 5%.

## 4. Split the game: small win, substantial month

The user’s split is right. High-frequency wins cannot be expensive or SKU-specific. The monthly crown can be an upsell door.

### Every winning run (small)

Job: they leave an email, they feel they won, they share the 15→12 gift. Not: they restock the café.

| Option | Looks like | Cost | SKU-agnostic | Cannibalises 10% welcome? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Current 5% / £100 | Coupon site | ~£5 if they order | Yes | **Yes — it is worse** |
| Unlock the existing 10% welcome by playing | Same as cart signup, earned | Same as today | Yes | No — it *is* the welcome |
| £15–20 off any order this month, cap ~50 | A twenty | Bounded | Yes | Only if you also leave 10% on the cart; pick one first-order discount |
| One free delivery, no £100 floor | Carriage on us | Postage | Yes | No |
| 15→12 gift to a mate | Status / generosity | ~0 | Yes | No |

**Recommendation for v1 of the split:** keep the **gift-link (15→12)** as the viral give. Make the personal win **one free delivery this month** (works for first *and* returning, SKU-agnostic, does not fight the 10%). Do not tell first-timers to play for 5% when the cart already has 10%. Do not “play to unlock 10%” either — returning buyers cannot have that 10%. Kill the extra MATE 5%.

Do not put a sample box on the win card. Point winners at `/samples` in the email if they have never ordered; that is follow-up, not the trophy.

### Stacker of the month (substantial)

Job: status + a door into a high-margin next step. One winner. Fulfil it in public or take it off the page.

**Preferred: “Your Name On It”** — shoutout from @afidasupplies **plus** first branded-print **setup waived** on any line the partner can print (cups, boxes, bags — they pick). They still buy the run. Afida eats origination, not a pallet.

Why this is the upsell Hormozi would recognise:

- Different *kind* of thing from the small win (not another discount).
- High perceived value (custom print is a grown-up buying decision).
- Contained cash cost (setup, not 10,000 units).
- Forces a conversation / configurator use / artwork — i.e. an engaged lead of a better class than a coupon.
- Matches the growth-plan wedge: print customer → packaging account.

If Brand Brothers owns print, the prize becomes a **cross-sell voucher into Brand Brothers**, still with the Afida shoutout. Decide that before the copy goes live.

**Fallback if print is politically messy:** a **free case of whatever they actually order** (they name the SKU from the last 90 days of the catalogue, or from their last invoice) **plus** the shoutout. Still SKU-agnostic. Still photographable. Still one winner. No print politics.

**Do not** promise “a free case of cups.” Pizza shops will remember.

### What the cabinet should say

- Menu: “Stack 15. Delivery on us, this month.” (or “unlock 10% on any order.”)
- Win card: the small prize + “forward this — they win at 12.”
- Board: “Stacker of the month: your logo on the packaging, setup on us, and a shoutout from @afidasupplies. Last month: @sohocoffee.”
- Share: lead with the gift, not the percentage.

---

## 5. Priority if only three things get built

1. **Stop selling 5% to first-timers** (the cart already has 10%). Keep a small, SKU-agnostic win that **returning** buyers can still use: one free delivery this month. If you keep a restock percentage at all, it is for people who already ordered, and it should look like pounds not 5%.
2. **Name the monthly crown as print-setup-waived + shoutout**, once leadership confirms where print lives. Until then, a free case of *their* line + shoutout. Put last month’s winner on the board.
3. **Put “Never Run Out” on the order-confirmation page** (continuity bonus). That is a bigger commercial offer than the game, and the game should not pretend to be it.

The game remains an advertisement. The offers above are the business.
