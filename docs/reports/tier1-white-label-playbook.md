# Tier 1 White-Label Playbook: UK Suppliers Who Will Print Afida's Brand

> **SUPERSEDED (2026-07-07) after Afida leadership review.** The premise was wrong for a supplier: low-MOQ retail-unit printing is a service for end customers (cafés wanting their logo on a cup), not for distributors, and Afida already has a print partner at good rates for that customer-facing work. Distributor own-label in this trade means **plain retail units with branded outer boxes and stamps, imported directly**; Enviropack (an existing Afida trading partner, not a cold lead as assumed here) runs exactly that model. Own-brand therefore goes straight to the direct-import path in the growth plan §5.1. This document is kept for reference: the RFQ question set, compliance checklist (certificate registers, DoC, EN 13432 verification) and pilot-gating logic transfer directly to direct-import due diligence.

_Prepared 2026-07-06. Deep-research output: 22 sources fetched, 25 top claims adversarially verified (3 independent votes each) against the suppliers' own live pages on 2026-07-06; 20 confirmed, 5 refuted. Everything below is marked verified, self-declared, or unverified. Companion to §5.1 of the growth plan (docs/reports/growth-plan-2026-2031.md)._

## The headline

Tier 1 is real and cheaper to start than assumed. At least five UK-based or UK-warehoused suppliers will custom-print their own stocked eco lines under Afida's brand, and for **cups** the printed MOQ floor is **500-1,500 units**, an order of magnitude below converter-scale floors (BioPak: 15,000 per cup size, 200,000 per size for straws and cutlery). A pilot run of Afida-branded double-wall hot cups is a **£500-1,500 decision**, not a £10k one.

The correct sequencing falls straight out of the published MOQs: **cups and boxes first**; napkins, straws and cutlery only later at volume, or printed via a distributor (Agora, Enviropack) rather than a converter.

## 2. The playbook, step by step

### Step 1: pick the pilot (one SKU, this month)

12oz double-wall hot cup, the proven bestseller. One size, one design, 1,000-5,000 units. Not a range; the pilot exists to answer "will customers buy the Afida label instead of Vegware", nothing else.

### Step 2: prepare the pack before contacting anyone

- Vector logo + simple wrap design (suppliers' in-house studios at Scyphus/Agora/Herald can finish it; don't gold-plate)
- Target spec written down: size, wall type, lining (ask for aqueous/PLA options and their certification status), colours (1-2 colours keeps origination cheap), quantity, delivery postcode
- Current Vegware landed cost per unit for the same SKU, so every quote has a benchmark

### Step 3: send the SAME spec to all five, simultaneously

Identical RFQ = comparable quotes. Ask every supplier the same list:

1. Printed MOQ for this exact SKU, and the plain (unprinted) MOQ for contrast
2. Unit price at 1,000 / 5,000 / 10,000, plain vs printed (the delta is the real cost of the brand)
3. Origination: plate/setup fees, per colour, and what's waived at what volume (Scyphus: free from 1,000; hold others to that)
4. Print method (flexo / offset / digital), colour limits, and a physical **printed pre-production sample** policy
5. Lead time from artwork approval, and restock lead time on repeat orders
6. Artwork process: dieline supplied? proof rounds included? who owns the plates?
7. **Certifications, with numbers:** EN 13432 / OK compost certificate number, who holds it (material maker, converter, or them), and written confirmation it covers the **printed** variant (ink type and coverage can narrow or void compostability certification)
8. Food-contact Declaration of Compliance (retained Regulation 1935/2004) naming the printed product, in writing
9. Stock-holding / call-off terms (Herald advertises this; ask everyone)
10. Exclusivity or territory expectations, if any; payment terms; price validity period (board prices move)

### Step 4: score and shortlist to two

Score on: printed unit cost vs current Vegware landed cost (the margin uplift must survive the print premium), origination cost, compliance paperwork actually produced (not promised), lead time, and stock-holding. Practitioner sources suggest printed MOQs often run ~2x the plain MOQ and setup fees of roughly £250-700 dominate small-run economics; treat those as negotiation context, not gospel (blog-grade sources).

### Step 5: verify compliance before money moves

- Certificate numbers checked against the issuer's public register (TÜV Austria, DIN CERTCO). This matters: verification found that distributor-level EN 13432/BRC statements are **routinely self-declared marketing copy**, and one shortlisted manufacturer publishes no certificate at all.
- Written DoC for the printed product in hand before the PO.
- Afida's own product-page wording drafted against CMA green-claims guidance ("industrially compostable where accepted", not "eco-friendly"); green-claims liability for what afida.com says is Afida's regardless of any supplier paperwork.
- **Open legal item, flagged honestly:** the exact statutory position on food-contact liability when a retailer white-labels a supplier's stocked line was NOT resolved by this research (the one claim on it was refuted in verification). England's food-contact regulations appear to bite on anyone placing product on the market, which would include Afida. Before the first PO, spend an hour with a trade-compliance advisor or the local Trading Standards business service to confirm what documentation Afida must itself hold. Budget line, not blocker.

### Step 6: pilot order and an honest test

1,000-5,000 units of the one SKU. Expected pilot spend £500-1,500 including origination (Nature's Cup's sub-10p/8oz anchor and Scyphus's free plates bracket this). Sell it on the same category page alongside the Vegware equivalent at a price that banks half the margin uplift and passes half to the customer. The test: **does the Afida-label cup reach ≥75% of the Vegware equivalent's velocity within 8-10 weeks?** Track it per-SKU (another reason COGS capture ships this year).

### Step 7: decision gate

- Pass → second SKU (8oz double-wall or kraft container), then negotiate repeat pricing and stock-holding; Tier 1 becomes a programme.
- Fail → the brand question is answered cheaply; stay a branded reseller, revisit after the trade-account base grows. No sunk pallets either way.

## 3. Pitfalls (each one traced to a verified or refuted finding)

- **Self-declared certificates.** Agora's EN 13432/BRC statements are their own copy; Nature's Cup publishes none; BioPak's custom-print page omits them. Certificate number + issuer register check, every time.
- **Printing can void the certificate.** A compostability certificate on the plain cup does not automatically cover a printed variant. Get the printed-variant confirmation in writing.
- **Too-good-to-be-true MOQs.** The one 100-piece option found was refuted on verification. Sub-500 printed cups basically don't exist; anyone offering it is printing a label, not the cup.
- **Advertised terms are opening positions.** Every MOQ/lead-time/price above is marketing copy verified as *displayed*, not as contracted, on 2026-07-06. Reconfirm at quote.
- **Range assumptions.** Two range-breadth claims failed verification (Enviropack's full coverage, Celebration Packaging as a candidate). Ask for the specific SKU; never assume from a category menu.
- **Napkins/straws/cutlery economics.** Converter floors are 30k-200k units. Don't burn credibility asking a converter to print 2,000 napkins; route those through a distributor's print service or leave them branded.
